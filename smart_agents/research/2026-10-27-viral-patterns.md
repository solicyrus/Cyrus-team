## Verifiable >1M-view examples

### 1. @VegasStarfish — MGM all-inclusive deal (TikTok) — 2,000,000 plays
- Stats exposed by the source: **2.0M plays, 86.1K likes,
  39.2K shares** (share rate is the standout — deal content
  gets forwarded to trip-planning friends).
- Hook (0–3s): a *news-style superlative claim* — "for the
  first time that I'm aware of, MGM is offering true
  all-inclusive packages". The first line is the whole story:
  clarity + surprise in one sentence.
- Pacing: single talking-head take, dense fact delivery, no
  B-roll cuts — proof that a strong claim can carry a plain
  frame when the information itself is the pattern interrupt.
- Payoff: the package contents are itemised (room + meals +
  show + attraction) — the promise is cashed in specifics.
- Loop device: none structural; the share-bait is the deal
  itself. Lesson for us: specifics + a real decision ("book
  this, not that") travel further than atmosphere.
- Source: https://www.tiktok.com/@vegasstarfish/video/7623243251234901278
  (canonical video URL; the play/like/share counts above are
  the ones the TikTok listing exposed to search on 2026-10-27)

### 2. The $13 MGM Grand Snickers (TikTok → X repost) — 1.2M views in <24h
- A 31-second single shot: creator holds up a candy bar in the
  MGM Grand gift shop and states its price; hook line:
  "People wonder why people aren't coming to Vegas."
- Hook type: *object-in-hand + price-shock + outrage question*
  ("Is that out of control or what?"). No editing tricks — the
  prop and the number ARE the pattern interrupt.
- Pacing: one continuous take; tension is the argument, not
  the cuts. Payoff = the comparison the audience completes
  themselves (the same bar costs a fraction elsewhere —
  stated by commentators, not the video).
- Loop device: ends on the question, so comments/replays
  re-argue it. Comment volume is the distribution engine.
- Lesson: a single concrete number + a relatable grievance
  beats production value. (Note for our fact gate: a stated
  price must be REAL and sourced — our scan would need the
  price to be verifiable, which is why our scripts keep place
  prices out unless approved.)
- Source: https://gamblerss.com/tv-producer-s-13-mgm-grand-candy-bar-video-becomes-his-biggest-hit-yet-57531

### 3. Sphere "dancing cats" clip (TikTok, via ARK Media) — 5,000,000 views
- Pure spectacle capture: the Sphere's LED exterior showing a
  bizarre animation, filmed from a parking lot; the creator's
  baffled reaction is the narration.
- Hook (0–3s): the *visual itself* — an already-moving,
  impossible-looking image in frame one. No setup, no intro.
- Pacing: one short clip, payoff is immediate and continuous;
  the loop is the replay — viewers rewatch to confirm what
  they saw. This is the strongest loop device in today's set:
  *confusion → rewatch*.
- Lesson: for visual-payoff content, start ON the payoff, not
  before it. Our 4-beat formula still applies — the hook beat
  must contain the strongest visual, not a wind-up.

## High-engagement Reels (social sweep; exact view counts not exposed by the platform)

### 6. "SECRET BAR IN LAS VEGAS AND HOW TO FIND IT" (Instagram reel)
- Opens on a sweeping night aerial of the Strip with bold
  yellow on-screen text stating the secret + the promise
  ("AND HOW TO FIND IT"), then numbered steps ("STEP 1: GO
  INSIDE…"). Hook = secret-reveal + a how-to promise; the
  step structure is the retention device (each step re-hooks).
- Payoff arrives only at the last step — classic open-loop
  pacing. Comments confirm the place is now crowded: the
  video's loop/step format drove real footfall.
- (Found via the team's social search sweep, 2026-10-27;
  reel in the sweep results from the Cosmopolitan/Ghost
  Donkey hidden-bar niche.)

### 7. Monorail pass savings reel (Instagram)
- Hook: *specific-number savings claim* — "my $14 24-hour
  pass saved me nearly $200", proven on camera by riding it.
  Tension = itemised proof (discounts listed one by one);
  payoff = the total. Debate in comments (free trams vs pass)
  keeps it circulating.
- Lesson: show the proof, don't assert it — our scripts should
  name the visible evidence beat by beat.

### 8. "7 Dumb Mistakes to Avoid in Las Vegas for First Timers" — Marco Murillo (Instagram, 2026-06-11)
- Numbered-list format with on-screen list; the countdown is
  the pacing engine (viewers wait for #1). Mistake/warning
  framing outperforms pure recommendation in the comments —
  viewers validate with their own anecdotes.
- Lesson: numbered tension ("mistake #3 is the expensive
  one") fits our Tension beat exactly.

## Cross-cutting formula evidence (craft sources)

- Shorts distribution rewards completion rate, rewatch rate
  and early engagement velocity; completion above ~85% is
  associated with breakout Shorts — which is why the hook and
  the loop are structural, not stylistic. On-screen text is
  critical (many viewers start muted).
  Source: https://www.whizsky.com/news/youtube-shorts-hooks-views
- Hook craft (Kallaway formula, 30M+ views/month): line one
  must (1) make the topic instantly clear and (2) give a
  reason to care (pain, benefit, or surprise); then a pattern
  interrupt word ("But", "However") re-fires attention.
  Worked Vegas example quoted there: "The tech in the Vegas
  Sphere is insane. Biggest screen ever built — 20x bigger
  than an IMAX."
  Source: https://writewithai.substack.com/p/this-youtube-hook-formula-generates

## Today's extracted pattern stack (what the Director must use)

1. Hook = clarity + surprise in ONE line, strongest visual
   first (no wind-up, no logo, no greeting).
2. A concrete anchor: a superlative, a number, a secret, or a
   numbered list — never a vague "amazing places" opening.
3. Middle = proof by showing, one new visual fact per line;
   pacing 1–3s per shot in montage sections.
4. Payoff lands at 15–25s, exactly what the hook promised.
5. Close = one decisive CTA/question, then the narrative loop
   line that re-enters the hook (rewatch by design — the
   Sphere-cats lesson: make the ending re-open the question).
